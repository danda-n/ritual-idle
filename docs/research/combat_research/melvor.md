# Melvor Idle combat (raw research report, 2026-09-29)

One of eight source reports behind [../COMBAT_RESEARCH.md](../COMBAT_RESEARCH.md), kept as written by the researcher. Unverified; see the evidence notes there.

## Summary
- The evidence here is mostly Steam discussions, Steam negative reviews and wiki text as quoted in search snippets. Search returned no Reddit posts, and direct wiki fetches returned HTTP 403. Reddit-specific claims below are therefore unverified.
- Melvor combat is a rock-paper-scissors triangle (melee, ranged and magic) plus accuracy and evasion. Players advise training all three styles rather than specializing (moderate evidence, one thread plus wiki).
- Auto Eat is the gate to idle combat. It is a costly shop upgrade, and it makes combat idleable when its threshold is above the monster's max hit. Early combat is described as "half idle" (single thread, consistent with the wiki).
- The death penalty is the most-cited pain point in the negative reviews I could read. Normal mode loses a random equipped item. Ammo, consumables and summoning tablets can be lost, but food and potions are not (wiki via snippet). Reviewers call this punishing.
- Combat is the weakest-loved pillar. Reviewers call combat "not very fun" and the "most frustrating to grind". Combat, slayer, dungeons and gearing were called the least fun parts (multiple threads, moderate evidence).
- Full idling is not possible early or in some late content without mods. This frustrates completionists more than casual players (single thread, echoed in reviews).
- The expansions got mixed reactions. The new skills (Archaeology, Cartography) were praised for utility passives. The combat additions were called over-tuned, with tanky barrier enemies (moderate evidence).
- The developer changed the Township and Skiller outfit task requirements from 95 to 85 tasks within two days of feedback (single thread). This is a responsiveness signal, not a combat lesson.

## Findings

**1. Combat triangle and style breadth**
- The triangle is melee over ranged, ranged over magic, and magic over melee. Hardcore and Adventure modes increase the penalty for a bad matchup.
- A player thread advises training all three styles because pure magic is weak against ranged enemies and evasive magic users. Defense and HP should be trained alongside the main style.
- Sources: https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Game_Mode, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3552805589781844277/
- Strength: moderate. The wiki was seen only through snippets, and the player advice comes from one thread.

**2. Auto Eat and idling**
- Auto Eat costs 1,000,000 GP in the shop. Idling works when the threshold is above the monster's max hit.
- Early combat is "half idle if you don't want to die". Players fight easy enemies such as cows while levelling. One player said only end-game content needs attention.
- Some late enemies apply stun, damage-reduction loss or affliction, which lowers the effective threshold.
- Sources: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/561358128180893038, https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/FAQ, https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Combat
- Strength: moderate. The claims repeat across threads and the wiki, but every source is a snippet or summary.

**3. Death, offline combat and loss**
- Normal mode loses a random item from the current equipment set on death. Protect Item prayer prevents this.
- Hardcore deletes the character and removes natural regen, so healing comes only from food or special abilities.
- Offline combat can end in death if a hit exceeds the threshold or food runs out. Offline combat is a setting that can be toggled.
- Offline progress is capped at 24 hours.
- Sources: https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/FAQ, https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Hardcore, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/591762563949134719
- Strength: moderate. A negative-review summary lists "item loss on death feels punishing" and harsh late-game dungeon death penalties (single review page).

**4. Slayer and dungeons**
- Slayer XP is 10% of the monster's max HP for killing the task target anywhere. Killing anything in a Slayer Area gives 5%.
- Dungeon monsters drop no loot and don't count for tasks. The dungeon gives a reward when the last monster dies.
- Source: https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Slayer, https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Combat (both via search snippets)
- Strength: wiki facts, not player sentiment.

**5. Progression pace**
- A player says combat skills are very slow to level: months of nonstop training barely reaching 120 in one skill.
- Another notes that level 100+ gear is gated behind an event regardless of skill level.
- A reviewer says the game "doesn't respect your time", with items taking weeks of farming.
- Sources: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3882723820583171054/, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/negativereviews/?l=german&browsefilter=toprated&snr=1_5_100010_
- Strength: moderate. Several anecdotes point the same way, but these are self-selected negative voices.

**6. Balance and item usefulness**
- One review says about 90% of potions are overshadowed by a few, such as damage reduction and slayer potions.
- Another thread notes poorly balanced monster drops for the base game, and that alternative items such as the clown hat make set bonuses irrelevant.
- Players want combat potions stackable, 3 to 5 at once, and say skilling potions offer more variety.
- Sources: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/677329263114868117/, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3811785047377336188 (potion claim from search snippet)
- Strength: weak to moderate, since these are individual posts.

**7. Combat level as a stat**
- Combat level is cosmetic and gives no bonuses. In Adventure mode it caps non-combat skills.
- Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3811785047377336188 (summary)
- Strength: single thread.

**8. Expansions**
- Throne of the Herald is recommended only for deep end-game players because all its content is post-max level. Atlas of Discovery is available earlier.
- Archaeology adds gear and a new equipment slot. Cartography adds broad passives but is called boring.
- The new combat is called over-tuned, and one commenter called it "nearly unplayable slow/boring without mods".
- Into the Abyss got mixed reactions. Abyssal levels are very slow, at 1.357M XP for the first level. Some players couldn't access it because their combat level was too low and asked for refunds.
- Sources: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3823048293516778888, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3882723164279193053, https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/4632611078379962566 (the last one appeared in search results but was not fetched)
- Strength: weak to moderate, and Into the Abyss is thin. I could not open primary threads on it.

## What players love
- Idle combat that finally runs unattended once Auto Eat and gear are set. The reward is a full day of farming (moderate evidence).
- Skilling that feeds combat, through food and equipment. Archaeology gear and Cartography passives are praised.
- Strong overall rating: 90 out of 100 from about 16k reviews (Steambase, https://steambase.io/games/melvor-idle/reviews).
- Developer responsiveness to feedback (single anecdote).
- Mods that make idling smooth are welcomed and officially supported (a mention in a thread).

## What players dislike / complaints
- Combat is the least fun part and the most frustrating to grind. Slow combat XP and multi-week farming were recurring complaints.
- The early game needs attention or you die, and full idling is not possible without mods.
- Item loss on death is punishing, especially in late-game dungeons.
- Combat feels like "2D statsheets" (a single review).
- Late enemies and barrier enemies feel tanky even with best-in-slot gear.
- Potion and item balance is uneven, with a few dominant choices.
- Gear is gated behind events or combat-level requirements, which locked some expansion buyers out.
- Melvor has server dependency in single-player, which is a general complaint and not about combat.

## Ideas and lessons for Ritual Idle
1. **Make Auto Eat a starter feature.** Melvor's Auto Eat is a costly shop purchase, and its absence is the source of the "half idle" early game. Ritual Idle could give an auto-use consumable rule at the start, such as "use a ward when HP is under X". It could be upgraded later with a threshold slider or smarter rules instead of being a gate.
2. **Match the designer's "active first playthrough, idle later" goal with preparation, not risk.** Early foes should be beatable with modest prep and should not need mid-fight tapping. The player's choice of loadout is the activity.
3. **Never lose items on death.** Melvor's random equipment loss is the most-cited grievance. Use a soft defeat instead: you retreat, lose some time or a few consumables, and heal by regen or items. This fits "you CAN lose" without RNG punishment.
4. **Offline combat should stop, not kill.** If a fight would be lost offline, end it with a "you withdrew" report and keep the loot so far. Add an offline "safe foes only" rule, where fights the loadout can't survive are skipped.
5. **Let the combat triangle become a warding triangle.** For example, salt against the restless dead, iron or candles against spirits, and herbs against curses. Show the matchup clearly in the UI so the player prepares instead of guessing. Avoid a punishing 'wrong style' penalty. Use a mild bonus for the right ward and a soft cap for the wrong one.
6. **Use skilling as the supply line.** Salt, candles and herbs used in combat become sinks that make crafting matter. Keep sinks modest so that gathering isn't a chore, and check the balance in `npm run pacing`.
7. **Avoid combat-only grind walls.** Melvor players complain about months of levelling and weeks of farming for items. Keep combat XP and drops in the pacing band the playthrough test enforces, and gate bosses by loadout, not by raw hours.
8. **Keep potions and consumables varied.** Melvor's complaint is that a few consumables dominate. Give each foe type a distinct best answer, so the loadout stays a real decision.
9. **Gates should be clear.** Melvor's cosmetic combat level and hidden event gates confuse players. If bosses unlock things, state exactly what a boss needs and what it opens.
10. **Bosses are where mid-fight switching lives.** Keep it optional for min-maxing, and make sure a preparation-only approach can still win with a better loadout.
11. **New terms need glossary entries.** Any new combat words (a wound, ward, banish and so on) go in `src/content/glossary.ts`, per the project rules.
