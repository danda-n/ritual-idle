# Slavic folklore bestiary and apotropaic methods (raw research report, 2026-09-29)

One of eight source reports behind [../COMBAT_RESEARCH.md](../COMBAT_RESEARCH.md), kept as written by the researcher. Unverified; see the evidence notes there.

## Summary

- Slavic folklore has no player-facing combat "stat blocks", but nearly every creature comes with a **specific counter** and a **specific seasonal or place trigger**. That is a natural fit for "prep, then auto-combat": you pick the right ward before you go.
- The best-evidenced counters, seen across many sources and in archaeology, are **sickle or iron, poppy seeds (make the dead count), salt and holy water, wormwood and other bitter herbs, red thread, and fire or candles**.
- Many enemies are not killed. They are **occupied, appeased, turned away or outwitted**: poppy counting, the noon witch's riddles, Dziady meals, bread for the domovoi. This suits a no-swords theme and "no RNG failure".
- The dead split into "dangerous dead" (upiór, strzyga, poroniec, utopiec, rusalka) and "ancestor dead" (Dziady). The same being can be a threat or an ally depending on how it was buried and fed. That gives a good design hook: banish some, feed others.
- Threat is **calendar- and place-bound**: rusalki during Green Week, the noon witch at midday in summer, Dziady in spring and autumn, water spirits in still water. These can be zones or modifiers without any real-time gating.
- Evidence quality varies. The Polish deviant-burial study and the Wikipedia entries rest on ethnographic and scholarly work. Many "Slavic lore" websites are popular and repeat each other. I flag which is which below.
- Gaps: one search was rate-limited, so noon-witch herbs (wormwood or mugwort) and vodník, leshy and bannik details lean on secondary sites. Treat those as leads to verify.
- This report is folklore research only. I found no player voices (Reddit, Steam, wikis) here, since that was outside my assigned area.

## Findings

Evidence strength: **Strong** means a scholarly source or several independent sources. **Moderate** means one reputable reference. **Weak** means a popular or blog source only.

### Theme 1: The graveyard and the unquiet dead

**Upiór / upir (vampire-like risen corpse).** It rises from the grave to drain the living. The strongest scholarly evidence is the 17th–18th century Drawsko cemetery in Poland. There, "deviant" burials had sickles placed across the throat or abdomen, and stones under the chin. Coins under the tongue were meant to give the dead something to bite. St. Benedict medals and crosses were also included. Isotope analysis showed the suspected people were locals, marked by manner of death or social suspicion, not outsiders.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4245124/ (Strong, peer-reviewed); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upi%C3%B3r (Moderate)
- Poppy seeds in the grave make the upiór count them all night until dawn. Popular sources repeat this. Aspen, hawthorn or other wooden stakes through the heart are also widely cited.
- Sources: https://darkslaviclore.com/myths/slavic-vampires; https://claramacgauffin.substack.com/p/upiors-vampires-before-dracula (Weak to Moderate; the poppy-counting motif recurs in many popular sources)
- Design note: the sickle, stones and coin are hard archaeological "wards". The counting compulsion is the best "no-violence" mechanic.

**Strzyga (double-souled, often female undead).** Marks include two hearts, two souls, two sets of teeth, no armpit hair, or being born with teeth. Counters listed: decapitating the corpse and burying the head apart, burying face down with a sickle round the head, and exhuming with a priest and putting a paper reading "Jesus" under the tongue. Also poppy seeds scattered in a cross in every corner of the house, and pealing church bells (she turns to tar). The Wikipedia page notes that after the 18th century the strzyga became more tied to witchcraft, and the upiór more of a flying vampire.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strzyga (Moderate); https://urbanlegendsmysteryandmyth.com/2025/09/the-strzyga-terrifying-vampire-of.html (Weak)

**Poroniec (from stillborn or improperly buried infants).** A malicious demon tied to pregnancy taboos. Wikipedia notes that burying the stillborn under the house threshold turns it into a protective house spirit (kłobuk) instead. So the same "dead" can become a foe or a guardian depending on the rite.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poroniec (Moderate)
- Design note: this is a strong "the ritual you perform decides what it becomes" concept.

### Theme 2: Water

**Rusalka (drowned young woman).** Most dangerous in Rusalka or Green Week (Trinity week, late May or June). She leaves the water to roam fields and forest and swing in birches. She lures young men and kills them by tickling or frenzied dancing. Counters cited: wormwood hung up, iron pins, the sign of the cross, avoiding swimming, and leaving certain work undone.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_water_spirits and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusalka_Week (Moderate for the creature and week). https://slaviclore.com/the-rusalka-and-the-green-week-festival (Weak for the wormwood and iron claims. The Wikipedia water-spirits page did not confirm those protections.)

**Vila.** Beautiful, winged, white-clad, mostly South Slavic, and generally benevolent but capricious.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_water_spirits (Moderate)

**Topielec / utopiec (drowned soul).** It lives in the water where it died and drags people into swamps and lakes. It also kills animals at still water.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topielec (Moderate; the page gave no counters).

**Vodník (Czech and Slovak) / vodyanoy (East Slavic).** Keeps drowned souls in pots. He passes as human but has a wet coat-tail that gives him away.
- Sources: https://prettymarginal.com/vodnik-the-water-spirit-and-the-cult-of-water/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodyanoy (Weak to Moderate)
- Design note: the "tell" (a wet coat-tail) suits a Grimoire discovery hook, where you learn how to recognise the enemy.

### Theme 3: House and hearth

**Domovoi (house spirit).** Usually benevolent but touchy. It is appeased with offerings such as porridge. Angry, it turns hostile. **Kikimora** is sometimes his wife. She breaks dishes and makes noise in an unkempt house, and a tidy house wins her favour (some sources say washing pots with fern tea calms her). **Bannik** (bathhouse) is "unclean" and may scald, suffocate or bind a bather. **Ovinnik** (threshing barn) gets pancakes and roosters as offerings.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domovoy; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikimora; https://russianlife.com/the-russia-file/dont-cross-the-domovoy/ (Moderate). The fern tea, rooster and pancake details come from blog and popular sources (Weak): https://storycrossroads.org/2025/04/12/k-kikimora-and-domovoi-slavic-folklore/
- Design note: the "restless domovoi" fits the house-defence and prep pillar. A neglected house makes tougher intruders.

### Theme 4: Night and nightmares

**Zmora / mora / nocnica.** The nightmare being that sits on sleepers' chests and legs. The scholarly angle is that it is often the wandering soul of a living, double-souled person (see the Folklore journal article). Counters: holy water by the bed, sleeping with an axe or other sharp metal, salt, dead magpies in stalls, sheep horns outside, fumigating the bedroom with a herb mixture, salty water sprinkled on animals, and changing sleeping place or position. Red ribbon or red hat protects children from certain demons.
- Sources: https://lamusdworski.wordpress.com/2015/10/28/polish-mythology-zmory/ (Moderate); https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0015587X.2022.2088957 (Strong; scholarly article, only the abstract was seen, not fetched). Red ribbon: https://thewickedgriffin.com/polish-folklore/ (Weak)

### Theme 5: Fields, forest and misfortune

**Południca / poludnitsa (the noon witch).** She appears at midday in summer as whirling dust, carrying a scythe or shears. She asks hard questions and punishes those who fail or change the subject. Punishments are decapitation, heatstroke, madness, headache or paralysis. The defence is conversation, or resting at the midday Angelus. Mugwort and wormwood as defences are unconfirmed in what I read.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poludnitsa (Moderate)
- Design note: it is a puzzle-style enemy. You answer or keep talking, not swing.

**Leshy (forest spirit).** Lord of beasts who misleads travellers. Counters cited: wearing clothes inside out or shoes on opposite feet to confuse his hold.
- Source: https://oldfolklore.com/leshy-the-guardian-spirit-of-the-forest/ (Weak)

**Likho (one-eyed misfortune).** It is fate, not a body. Folklore says you cheat it, or pass it on with a gift. Drowning it fails, and greed re-catches you. A proverb warns against waking it.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likho (Moderate)
- Design note: a "curse" enemy fits a debuff-cleansing mechanic.

### Theme 6: Dziady (Forefathers' Eve)

Held twice a year, in spring (about 1–2 May) and autumn (31 Oct–1 Nov). Families left food and drink (honey, groats, eggs, kutia, vodka) on the table or at the grave, and shared portions with the dead. Doors were left open. Fires and candles lit the way home for souls, and were also said to keep malevolent spirits (suicides, the drowned) from manifesting. Beggars called *dziady* took food and donations in return for prayers, acting as intermediaries. Taboos: no loud noise, no clearing the table after supper, no pouring dishwater out the window, no sewing, weaving or flax work, and no fist on the table. All of these were meant to avoid trapping or disturbing souls.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dziady (Moderate); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dziady_(wandering_beggars); https://yourrootsinpoland.com/dziady-supernatural-genealogy/ (Weak)
- The turning-away half is thinner in my sources. Fire and the taboos are documented. A formal "send-off" incantation was not confirmed by what I read.

## What players love

No player voices were collected here. The following is not evidence of what players love, only what the folklore offers a designer:
- Enemies with clear, checkable counters (poppy, sickle, iron, wormwood) rather than raw HP. This is a design inference, not a sourced finding.
- Recognition and reveal (the wet coat-tail, born with teeth). This suits a Grimoire discovery model.

## What players dislike / complaints

None collected in my area. Folklore-specific cautions:
- Sources disagree and blogs repeat unverified claims. The wormwood and iron protections against rusalki and the "Jesus" paper against strzygi come from popular sources, so avoid presenting them as scholarly fact.
- The Christian layer (crosses, holy water, church bells, "Jesus" paper) sits awkwardly with the pagan-occult tone. It is your call whether to reskin it as candles, salt and sigils.

## Ideas and lessons for Ritual Idle

These respect your rules: no real-time gating, no failure punishment, generous offline progress, no pay-to-win.

1. **Enemy families with "ward types".** Group enemies by where they live (Hearth, Graveyard, Field, Forest, Water, Night). Each family has a **weakness ward** made from Chapter 1 goods:
   - Dead (upiór, strzyga): poppy (the counting ward), iron or sickle, salt.
   - Water spirits (rusalka, utopiec): wormwood, red thread.
   - Night (zmora): salt water, sharp iron, herb smoke.
   - Curses (likho): a gift-charm that passes the curse on.
   - House spirits (domovoi, kikimora): offerings and tidiness, not killing.
2. **Counting as a mechanic.** A poppy ward makes an undead enemy "count" and waste time. Suggested effect: the enemy skips or delays attacks, or stops fighting for a while. This is the most distinctive, non-violent counter in the folklore.
3. **Sigils do the "iron" work.** Since there are no swords, a sickle or iron sigil could be the damage source. A salt line stops movement. A candle lights the way and weakens night enemies. Fire and candles are attested by Dziady.
4. **Two ways to win: banish or appease.** Ancestors and domestic spirits (domovoi, Dziady dead) are fed. Hostile ones are warded off. Feeding the right ones could give buffs, and this ties into the existing Rite.
5. **Seasonal and place modifiers as content rotation, not real-time gates.** Rusalki in "Green Week", the noon witch in "midday" zones, Dziady in "autumn and spring" zones. Make these zones or in-game phases the player enters by choice, not by wall-clock waits.
6. **Loadout depth comes from matching, not stats.** A prep screen where you slot a ward, a light, and an offering for the enemy family gives "simple yet deep". Wrong wards should slow you, not fail you.
7. **Bosses as riddle or rite fights.** The noon witch (answer her, no swings), likho (redirect the curse), a strzyga (a multi-step counter such as decapitate, sickle, poppy). They map naturally to your "mid-fight switching for bosses".
8. **Offline combat.** Weak spirits (nocnica, a restless domovoi) have easy fixed counters. Let the auto-fight run offline against enemies your loadout already fully counters.
9. **Discovery hook.** Enemy "tells" (wet coat-tail, born with teeth, no armpit hair) can feed the Grimoire. The player learns an enemy's weakness by meeting it, which fits the discovery and hint model.
10. **The poroniec transformation.** A burial choice turns a hostile spirit into a house guardian. It is a good one-off story beat or an unlock.
11. **Christian elements** could be reskinned as neutral ritual items. Salt, candles, bells and herbs cover the same ground.
