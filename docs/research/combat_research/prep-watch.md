# Prep-then-watch depth (raw research report, 2026-09-29)

One of eight source reports behind [../COMBAT_RESEARCH.md](../COMBAT_RESEARCH.md), kept as written by the researcher. Unverified; see the evidence notes there.

## Summary
- Evidence is a mix of fetched player threads (Steam), reviews and web-search summaries. Several games in the brief (The Bazaar, Idle Slayer, Nodebuster, Dungeon Clawler) got only thin or off-topic results. I could not reach Reddit or the wikis through these tools, so no Reddit evidence is included.
- Prep-then-watch depth comes from a small number of interacting variables that the player arranges. Loop Hero (tile placement plus stat synergy), Backpack Battles (item position plus adjacency) and Unnamed Space Idle (which weapons for which sector) all fit this.
- Arranging with no time pressure is itself a draw. Backpack Battles is praised for auto-battle with unlimited arranging time, and Soda Dungeon for being playable while busy.
- The clearest depth failure is that choices feel meaningless. Loop Hero critics say the game "plays for you", and Despot's Game players report a narrow set of winning compositions. Both are single-thread or review-level evidence.
- The second failure is RNG that decides outcomes. Backpack Battles ("best build is based on luck of the store"), Super Auto Pets (luck ranked over skill), Despot's Game (mutation luck) and Dungeon Clawler (claw RNG) all draw this complaint. It matters here because of your "no RNG punishing the player" rule.
- Counters and rock-paper-scissors dynamics come across as fun when the player can see the enemy and prepare for it. They feel unfair when the opponent is hidden (Backpack Battles async PvP, Super Auto Pets counter-picks). Unnamed Space Idle and Loop Hero's Vampire Mansion swamp counter are the visible, prep-able version.
- Grinding gear through RNG drops is a complaint in Idle Slayer's Armory, and players who wanted idle play disliked being pushed into active play for armor. This is one anecdote-level source, but it matches your design rules.

## Findings

**1. Depth from adjacency and placement (Loop Hero, Backpack Battles)**
- Loop Hero's tile synergies are placement-driven. The Vampire Mansion transforms neighbouring Villages into Ransacked Villages that spawn ghouls, and it turns Bookery tomes into Vampire Mages. A Swamp beside a Mansion makes vampire healing hurt the vampires. Source: https://loophero.fandom.com/wiki/Synergy, via search summary (page itself returned 402 to fetch).
- A Steam thread describes stat synergy as the core (e.g. stack counterattack-heal with counter items) and class-specific stat priorities. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1282730/discussions/0/3112522283883159725
- A Gigazine reviewer says Backpack Battles is left/right-click simple, yet synergy positioning makes you "discover new things every time". Early play can feel monotonous, and depth grows as the inventory grows. Source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240310-backpack-battles/
- Strength: moderate. It is consistent across sources, but each is one thread or review.

**2. "The game plays itself" is a real divide (Loop Hero)**
- One critic in a Steam thread says even at double speed it "basically plays for you". Defenders reply that limited control is the concept and that there is hidden depth. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1282730/discussions/0/4768721792061467717
- Another thread (via search summary) says placement such as where you put a cemetery hardly matters if you use common sense. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1282730/discussions/0/3114771547431043349 (search-summary only).
- Strength: moderate, since both sides are visible. The sharpest critique is that many choices are low-stakes.

**3. Unlimited prep time is a feature (Backpack Battles, Soda Dungeon)**
- Backpack Battles: auto-battle removes real-time pressure, and a 2x speed option keeps the loop fast. Source: the Gigazine review above.
- Soda Dungeon: auto mode is described as the optimal way to play and good "when you are busy". A preset loadout upgrade saves equipping time, but combat itself is called shallow and dungeons repetitive. Source: https://rpgranked.com/soda-dungeon-review/
- Strength: moderate.

**4. Loadout matched to enemy or sector (Unnamed Space Idle)**
- The store page says weapons and defenses are tailored to counter specific enemy types. Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/2471100/Unnamed_Space_Idle/ (search summary).
- A Steam thread on getting past sector 28 shows the pattern. Players advise not mixing redundant weapons, putting main DPS in front slots, retrofitting as you go, and using a specific disruptor setup for phased-ship bosses. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/2471100/discussions/0/3812913565880031464/
- This is one thread, but it is the closest match to your "prep then auto-fight until you stop" model. The guide page (spaceidle.game-vault.net) returned 403, so I could not verify the detail.
- Strength: moderate. The game has 92% positive reviews out of about 3,000 (search summary).

**5. RNG in the build undermines "prep" (Backpack Battles, Super Auto Pets, Despot's Game, Dungeon Clawler)**
- Backpack Battles: one complainant says there is "absolutely zero strategy outside of just building up". Others say hidden opponents make counter-building impossible. Defenders say top players manage RNG and economy. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/2427700/discussions/0/4333103687427350925/
- Super Auto Pets: a thread ranks luck first, meta-awareness second and skill third. Players describe "rock-paper-scissors" counter feelings and say experimenting off-meta is punished. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1714040/discussions/0/3272435584435760121/
- Despot's Game: reviews (search summary) say progression depends too much on getting the right mutations. They also say strategies are limited, and that content gets stale fast. Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1227280/Despots_Game_Dystopian_Army_Builder/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/1227280/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated
- Dungeon Clawler: claw RNG is a consistent complaint, and useful items get buried. Reviewers also praise its combos and build variety. Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/dungeon-clawler-is-a-roguelike-claw-machine-game-which-is-just-as-frustrating-and-moreish-as-it-sounds/ (search summary).
- Strength: strong as a pattern, because it recurs across four games. Individual claims are anecdotal.

**6. Meta staleness and solved builds**
- Super Auto Pets: "same boring OP builds" with a stale meta (search summary of Steam reviews). Despot's Game players say the meta "smashes your face in" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1227280/discussions/0/3812911928397734308/). Both are competitive PvP games, where solving is driven by other players. That partly does not apply to a single-player PvE game, though a fixed enemy roster can be solved the same way.
- Strength: moderate, and mostly about PvP.

**7. Gear grind and forced activity (Idle Slayer)**
- Search-summary evidence that the Armory drops feel like an "RNG slog", and that some idle players find it boring or pushed toward active play. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1353300/negativereviews/ (via search).
- Strength: weak-to-moderate (one summarised source), but it lines up with your rules.

**8. Length and focus (Nodebuster)**
- Nodebuster gets 97% positive from about 10,600 reviews. Reviews praise clear, streamlined upgrade paths and a roughly 4-hour "all-killer-no-filler" length. Source: https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/ (search summary).
- It is a different design from prep-then-watch, so the evidence is thin. Its lesson is clarity, not depth.
- Strength: weak for your question.

## What players love
- Arranging with no time pressure and a fast-forward option (Backpack Battles).
- Discovering an interaction (synergy) yourself, especially one tied to position or adjacency (Loop Hero, Backpack Battles).
- Auto-play that can be left alone while busy (Soda Dungeon).
- No microtransactions and no pay-to-win (Backpack Battles reviews, via search summary).
- Loadouts that reward knowing the enemy (Unnamed Space Idle sector setups).
- Retreating early and banking loot as a smart, learnable choice (Loop Hero: "dont be a hero" thread).

## What players dislike / complaints
- Choices that feel cosmetic, so the game plays itself (Loop Hero critics).
- Opponents or drops decided by luck, so a good build fails through no fault of the player (Backpack Battles, Super Auto Pets, Despot's Game, Dungeon Clawler).
- Hidden information that stops you from countering (Backpack Battles async PvP).
- A narrow set of winning compositions or a stale meta (Despot's Game, Super Auto Pets).
- Shallow combat and repeated content once the loadout is set (Soda Dungeon review).
- Gear-farming that feels like an RNG slog (Idle Slayer).
- Equipment management chores. Soda Dungeon added a preset loadout to fix this, which implies the chore was real.

## Ideas and lessons for Ritual Idle
- **Make prep the game, and keep slots few.** Give 3-5 loadout slots (charm, ward, candle, salt, herb or sigil), each with real trade-offs. Loop Hero and Unnamed Space Idle show that few, meaningful slots plus visible enemy types do the work. Avoid many slots with marginal bonuses, which is the "data entry" complaint.
- **Use adjacency or pairing for depth.** For example a candle plus salt in the same ward gives a bonus, herb plus sigil transforms. This is Loop Hero's synergy, and it is teachable through the Grimoire discovery model. A small number of readable recipes beats large stat math.
- **Show enemy types before the fight.** A spirit's weakness (salt, iron, fire, running water, bells) should be visible or learnable, so prep beats a hidden counter. Pair this with the Grimoire hint model. This gives you Unnamed Space Idle's "right tool for the sector" without Backpack Battles' hidden-opponent frustration.
- **Deterministic prep, no loot-shop RNG.** Give the player the crafted goods deliberately (crafting is your existing skilling loop) instead of a random shop. That answers the RNG complaint seen in four games and matches your no-RNG-failure rule. Keep any randomness in cosmetic or flavour outcomes, seeded through `state.rngSeed`.
- **Make losing safe and readable.** HP drops, and the fight ends in a retreat rather than a wipe, with limited loss (for instance a few consumables). Loop Hero's "retreat with a smaller share" is the model. This supports offline play against weaker foes.
- **Offline runs use the same rule as `advance`.** Tell the player when the loadout no longer beats the zone. A "you'd win/lose" estimate in the prep screen makes the math less opaque.
- **Add a loadout preset.** Soda Dungeon added it to cut the equipping chore, and presets keep the no-management-chores rule. Let the player save one loadout per enemy family.
- **Keep the gear grind crafted, not dropped.** Idle Slayer's Armory complaint suggests drop-only gear feels like a slog. Use drops for materials and let crafting decide the gear.
- **Bosses as the depth test.** Bosses where the player must read a weakness and prep specifically (a Rite-like challenge) give self-challenge and unlocks. Loop Hero and Unnamed Space Idle's phased bosses both use this shape.
- **Guard against solved builds.** Rotate what works by zone (different unquiet dead resist different goods), so one best loadout doesn't cover everything. Consider a small cost or cooldown-free trade-off (each ward slot eats a consumable) so the "best" set is not free.
- **Mid-fight swapping stays optional.** No source contradicts your plan that swapping is for min-maxing and bosses. The evidence says players like watching once the prep is done.
