# Incremental and active-idle RPGs (raw research report, 2026-09-29)

One of eight source reports behind [../COMBAT.md](../COMBAT.md), kept as written by the researcher. Unverified; see the evidence notes there.

## Summary
- Coverage is thin. Search returned mostly Steam threads, wikis and guides, and no Reddit posts. I did not research Increlution, Idle Wizard, Grim Tides, Progress Knight/Quest or Kittens Game. Trimps player sentiment came back empty. Treat everything below as directional.
- **Active play buys speed, and idle play is a fallback.** NGU Idle's guide says manual fighting is much stronger than Idle Attack. Idle Attack is described as the "AFK fallback". Titans are killed manually early, and they unlock features. (Strong, but one guide plus Steam threads.)
- **Melvor players describe a ramp.** Combat starts active, then auto-eat and stocked food make even hard content idle-able. Players say the game does not punish closing it. Bosses and dungeons stay the most active part. (Moderate: several Steam posters.)
- **Offline and auto kills of gated content give the same rewards as manual kills.** In NGU Idle, auto-kill of Titans while the game is open, and offline kills, pay the same as manual kills. Players see auto as a benefit, not a loss. (Anecdotal, but a clear statement.)
- **Prep is the deep part in Trimps, Idle Champions and Magic Research 2.** Trimps has breed speed, health and heirloom prep. Idle Champions has formations. Magic Research 2 has spells and wizard staffing. The most-cited pain point is repeated re-setup: Idle Champions reviewers complain that re-optimising teams is tedious and automated formations don't hold their settings. (Moderate.)
- **Opaque hidden drop logic irritates players.** In IdleOn, players suspect hidden factors behind zero-drop AFK stretches. (Single thread, anecdote.)
- **Walls plus monetisation is the worst-reviewed combination.** Idle Champions negative reviews cite hard progress walls that need grinding or paying, and a "business model, not a game" feel. This is a sign of what to avoid. (Single review page, but a clear pattern.)
- **Community automation appears where the game demands too much.** Trimps has the AutoTrimps script, and its maintainers warn it isn't meant for new players. That suggests the game has repeated chores worth building in-game automation for. (My inference from the script's existence.)

## Findings

**1. Idle mode is weaker than manual mode, and manual is reserved for gates (NGU Idle)**
- Idle Attack is always unlocked and ticks once per second. Manual play is "significantly stronger". The guide recommends manual play for Titans, for targeting bosses for gear, and for climbing ITOPOD. It says gear from adventure farming is the main progression path. https://sayolove.github.io/ngu-guide/en/mechanics/adventure/
- Steam players agree that Titans are much easier manually, with a buff-and-ultimate combo. Manual play also lets you heal 15% HP on demand instead of waiting for regen. Beast Mode is a risk/reward toggle: +40% power, +300% damage taken. You turn it off before a Titan's first hit. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/discussions/0/1749024925637733504/ and https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/discussions/0/3789254082586144744/
- ITOPOD community advice is to farm an optimal floor where you one-hit enemies, not the deepest floor. That is efficiency-driven, "stay put and farm" play. Sources are Steam threads surfaced by search: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/discussions/0/3492004959421511945 and others.
- Evidence strength: moderate to strong for the manual-versus-idle gap (a guide plus threads). Weak for player feelings about it. I found no direct complaint about tedium.

**2. Auto-kill and offline kills match manual rewards (NGU Idle)**
- A Steam thread says offline kills happen regardless of the toggle. It says auto-kills give the same drops as manual kills. The only reason to turn auto-kill off is save-scumming. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/discussions/0/4349865446111093857/
- Strength: anecdotal, but stated clearly.

**3. Combat as a ramp from active to idle (Melvor Idle, as a comparison)**
- A poster says combat is active until you unlock Auto Eat. Another says "Almost everything can be done AFK". Another says you can fight beyond your current limits by actively participating. Offline progress is described as simulated accurately and not punished. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/591762563949134719
- Other threads say that with auto-eat and gear you can leave the game for a day. Mid-game dungeons need more involvement. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3764482197525821795
- Strength: several posters agree. This is the model closest to the designer's intent.

**4. Prep and loadout depth**
- Trimps rewards preparation: map farming for upgrades (maps unlock at zone 6), breed speed and health before hard content such as the Spire, and heirloom choices. https://trimps.fandom.com/wiki/Guide:How_to_attempt_the_Spire and https://trimps.fandom.com/wiki/Player_Guide_(Ells). This comes from wiki guides, not player opinion.
- Magic Research 2: reviews describe combat as automatic, with spells you can cast to change the outcome or leave to fellow wizards. Reviews call the staffing trade-offs a design highlight. https://playwanderer.online/game-reviews/magic-research-2 and https://expertgamereviews.com/magic-research-2-review-a-spellbinding-incremental-rpg-adventure/. The Steam Community page shows 92% of about 610 reviews positive per the search summary, not verified directly (https://store.steampowered.com/app/2864890/Magic_Research_2/).
- A Steam poster says familiars seem pointless early but become viable after unlocking death and hidden classes. Another says automated crafting can brick mana regen if production doesn't match consumption. https://steamcommunity.com/app/2864890/discussions/0/6193092963016494944
- One Magic Research 2 poster wants a combat log because spells feel like they "don't really matter". That is a legibility problem.
- Strength: moderate. The evidence is mostly reviews and wikis, with few player quotes.

**5. Setup friction (Idle Champions)**
- A search summary of Steam threads reports that formation automation sometimes fails to swap champions between runs. It also says saved specialisations in the modron core have to be reselected. https://steamcommunity.com/app/627690/discussions/0/1642045003571394503 and others surfaced by search. I did not open these threads myself.
- Negative reviews say it "takes a ton of time to manually optimise" teams each visit. They also complain of hard walls and predatory monetisation. https://steamcommunity.com/app/627690/negativereviews/?p=1&browsefilter=toprated. Overall the game is 76% positive of about 9,565 reviews, per the search summary.
- Click damage is discussed as weak late-game. Active play is used mainly for boss assists, such as a fire-breath potion. https://steamcommunity.com/app/627690/discussions/0/5015307809833051394. This is thread-title-level evidence from search summaries.
- Strength: moderate. It repeats across several threads, but I read few of them in full.

**6. Hidden drop rules (IdleOn)**
- An AFK-versus-fighting thread shows players guessing at undisclosed multipliers and time thresholds behind zero-drop stretches. One poster says drop rate is meaningless if an undisclosed factor blocks drops. Another suggests collecting AFK gains every 30 to 60 minutes. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1476970/discussions/0/599639412970623691/
- The IdleOn wiki notes AFK fighting has a gain cap, near 85% of active. Rare skilling drops can't roll while active. https://idleon.wiki/wiki/Game_Mechanics_AFK. This is a wiki page and the search summary is not fully verified.
- Strength: one thread, anecdotal. It fits a broader pattern of players disliking opaque RNG.

**7. Automation mods and scripts (Trimps)**
- The community AutoTrimps script automates map farming, heirloom upgrades and pre-Spire farming. It warns it is not meant for players under zone 60. https://github.com/genbtc/AutoTrimps and others.
- Trimps' Steam page shows 90% positive of about 1,496 reviews and says no spam clicking is needed. https://store.steampowered.com/app/1877960/Trimps/
- Strength: weak evidence of player opinion. The script's existence is a fact. My reading that players want to skip chores is an inference.

## What players love
- A real choice between playing actively for speed and stepping away safely. Melvor threads show this most clearly.
- Prep-heavy games where the setup is the puzzle, and the fight then runs itself (Magic Research 2, Trimps, Idle Champions formations).
- Progress that isn't lost by closing the game. Melvor posters say closing it isn't punished.
- Bosses as gates that reward you with a feature unlock (NGU Titans). Manual play is an optional accelerator, and offline kills still pay out.
- Risk/reward toggles inside a simple system (NGU Beast Mode).

## What players dislike / complaints
- Repeated re-setup when nothing has changed (Idle Champions formations, per reviews and threads).
- Hard walls that push toward grinding or spending (Idle Champions negative reviews).
- Hidden drop logic and unexplained zero-drop stretches (IdleOn thread).
- Combat effects that feel like they don't matter (a Magic Research 2 thread poster wanting a combat log).
- Early-game combat that needs babysitting before healing automation exists (Melvor posters, mild).
- Automation that fails to hold saved settings (Idle Champions threads).

## Ideas and lessons for Ritual Idle
1. **Ramp from active to idle by unlocking auto-healing.** Early on, the player watches HP and uses an item to heal. Later a crafted ward (say, a charm that auto-uses a poultice) makes safe zones fully idle. This mirrors Melvor's auto-eat, and it keeps the first playthrough active without a real-time wait.
2. **Reward active play with speed and access, not with better loot.** Follow NGU Idle: stay-put farming is the default, and the top tier of content is reachable only by prepping and being present, such as bosses and "just beyond your limit" foes. Offline and auto results should pay the same drops as attended play, per the NGU thread, to respect the generous-offline rule.
3. **Make bosses feature gates.** A defeated boss opens a place, recipe or talent (the NGU Titan pattern), so a boss is never a wall you can only pass by grinding. If a boss is hard, prep crafting is the answer, not spending or time.
4. **Make prep the game, and save it.** Store loadouts as named presets (charms, sigils, consumables), and remember the last loadout per foe or place. Idle Champions' worst friction is re-optimising each visit. Offer a "same as last time" button, and never reset choices on a run restart.
5. **Show the maths.** Tell the player why a fight is safe or dangerous ("your salt ward blocks X of their hits"), and add a short combat log. This answers the "spells don't matter" complaint and IdleOn's hidden-multiplier suspicion. Keep drop rules visible and avoid hidden gating thresholds.
6. **Keep the fight itself low-input.** Tie mid-fight actions to a few risk/reward toggles at bosses, on the model of Beast Mode: for example lighting a candle for more damage but taking more harm. These stay optional min-max tools and never change the outcome of a fight the player is over-prepared for.
7. **Softer failure.** Losing should cost the fight's consumables or a short retreat, not progress. Auto-retreat at low HP with a summary, so offline combat against weaker foes is safe. This fits the no-RNG-punishment rule. I have no direct source on retreat design from these games.
8. **Avoid walls that only grinding or money pass.** Give each wall a crafting-based solution, such as a specific sigil or salt tier that the Grimoire hints at. A wall should say what it wants.
9. **Consider an in-game automation tier for repeat chores.** The existence of AutoTrimps suggests players will automate upkeep. Offer "auto-restock consumables from the workshop" as an unlock, so it isn't a management chore.
10. **Multiple action slots later.** Combat takes the single slot at first. Followers or extra slots then let a second activity run during a fight, the way Magic Research 2 lets you assign wizards to jobs. Watch for the mana-brick problem a poster describes: two automated loops competing for one resource.
