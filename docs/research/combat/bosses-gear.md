# Bosses, gates, gear tiers and parallel actions (raw research report, 2026-09-29)

One of eight source reports behind [../COMBAT.md](../COMBAT.md), kept as written by the researcher. Unverified; see the evidence notes there.

## Summary
- Bosses work best in idle games as gates. A stuck player thinks "I need upgrades to beat this." That is the healthiest loop the sources describe. Evidence: several single sources, one repeated pattern (Melvor dungeons, NGU titans, Magic Research bosses).
- Melvor's dungeon gates are the strongest data point. Beating a dungeon unlocks equippable gear, but players resent it when they already own the item. A commenter defended the gate as standard "grind gear, clear dungeon, gain gear, repeat."
- Hard bosses are where the "idle it" promise breaks. On Melvor's hardest dungeon, players report 0 deaths/hr with manual eating versus about 1.24 deaths/hr idling. Melvor's Herald boss has phase-based counters, and a wiki guide says pets can make it idle-able (clear time about 2 hours down to 15 minutes). Players accept this as endgame. It was only reported for the hardest content.
- Gear obsolescence hurts most when new content out-crafts a hard-earned item. Melvor players complained that the DLC lets you craft a better weapon than their hard-earned base-game ultimate. IdleOn players say earlier boss armor stays viable a world or two later (only one thread), and that stat bonuses from stamps, cards and alchemy matter more than the top armor tier.
- Parallel actions are contested. Melvor's developers firmly reject "two actions at once," and the request comes up "every month or so." IdleOn allows only 1 active character and up to 10 AFK ones, and its devs treat botting multiple actives as cheating. Increlution uses a sequential queue rather than parallel actions and is praised for cutting friction.
- Loadouts are how idle games let players prep without micromanaging. NGU Idle players keep several named equipment sets, each for one activity (gold, power/toughness, farming). Veterans say combined loadouts with small efficiency loss are fine.
- Search tools were rate-limited midway. No Reddit threads came back directly, and Steam discussions and wikis were the main sources. Several other topics (Idle Clans specifics, Anuke-style games, Magic Research parallelism) have thin or no evidence here.

## Findings

### 1. Bosses as progression gates
- **Boss as "a wall that sends you shopping."** A jam-game commenter recommends difficult bosses as gates over "more enemies," and the dev agreed. Source: https://itch.io/post/15511691. Strength: single anecdote, but consistent with the rest.
- **NGU Idle's boss ladder is not the real progress measure.** There are 301 bosses per difficulty, and players say bosses unlock faster than the player can reach adventure zones or Titans. Advice quoted: "Don't focus on bosses." Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/discussions/0/2967272318174411940. Strength: single thread. It suggests a long boss ladder feels like filler when it isn't tied to a meaningful unlock.
- **Boss defeat unlocks a whole mode.** NGU's Adventure mode is unlocked by defeating boss 4. Source: https://ngu-idle.fandom.com/wiki/Adventure_Mode. Strength: wiki fact.
- **Loop Hero: unprepared boss encounters punish hard.** One player lost "70% of your stuff" repeatedly after triggering the Lich early. Responders explained the boss gauge and auto-pause as ways to avoid it. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1282730/discussions/0/3278066986681565260. Strength: single anecdote. It shows a boss you can trigger by accident, plus an unclear penalty, is a bad combination.
- **Magic Research: "mostly idle until you hit a boss."** Bosses "often require strategy and paying attention." Players present this as what makes it suit people who dislike pure idlers. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/2311680/discussions/0/3874843885717583371/. Strength: single thread, positive framing.

### 2. Boss mechanics that demand prep or switching
- **Melvor's hardest dungeon can't be idled on a first clear.** It deals 1-7500 damage that ignores damage reduction. Community prep advice: HP investment, a Wasteful Ring (raises the auto-eat threshold), potions, and manual eating. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/570371033831351972/. Strength: one thread with several consistent replies.
- **Phase-based bosses reward switching to a counter style.** The Herald uses melee, then ranged, so the guide says use the combat triangle to counter each phase. Source: https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Throne_of_the_Herald/Guide. Strength: wiki guide. Pets can make the boss idle-able, cutting clear time from about 2 hours to 15 minutes, so long-term collection rewards prep-light play.
- **IdleOn dungeons.** A thread suggests speed potions in boss fights and turning off auto mode for manual control in hard encounters. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1476970/discussions/0/3764480479602641804/. Strength: single thread.

### 3. Timed versus untimed bosses
- I found no direct evidence about timers versus no timers. The Loop Hero and Melvor examples both involve being ground down by damage, not a clock. Treat this area as an unanswered question.

### 4. Gear tiers, obsolescence and gating
- **Melvor's gear is crafted (Smithing metals) up to a point, and top gear is drop-based.** Mithril is smithed and upgraded with bars. Dragon gear is drop-based. Dungeon chests give gear like Ancient Armor. Sources: https://earlyguides.com/melvor-idle/weapons and https://shapes.inc/fandom/melvor-idle/items-and-equipment (secondary guide sites, not primary voices).
- **Complaint: gear you own but can't equip.** A player spent three weeks farming an item that required clearing Impending Darkness first. Some agreed. A defender said the gate exists to stop DLC gear being pay-to-win. Balance concerns were raised too (magic dominates). Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3735205021140855117/?l=english. Strength: one thread, with real division.
- **Complaint: new content makes old ultimate gear obsolete.** Preference stated: upgrade paths over replacement. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/3487500856965647747/. Strength: one thread.
- **IdleOn: gear matters less than accumulated bonuses.** "Boss armor is generally better and easier to get" than climbing every metal tier. Players advise stamps, cards and alchemy over gear grinding, and note "1% stacks greatly." Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1476970/discussions/0/5153834725007019951/?ctp=2. Strength: one thread.
- **Multiverse Loot Hunter and Firestone.** Search summaries say old weapons become obsolete when new zones open, though older set bonuses still matter, and Firestone's higher tiers benefit all heroes. Sources: https://steamcommunity.com/app/2776450/discussions/0/603021022325697319 and https://firestone-idle-rpg.fandom.com/wiki/Gear. I did not open these pages, so treat them as weak.

### 5. Crafting-fed gear
- Melvor is the clearest example of skilling making gear. I found no primary player quotes praising or criticizing this specifically.
- Idle Clans advertises smithing armor, crafting weapons, enchanting jewellery and brewing potions before fights. Source: https://www.idleclans.com/idle-mmorpg/. It has a "Mostly Positive" score of 74% from 1,686 reviews, and some reviewers say content runs thin when active. Source: https://store.steampowered.com/app/2103530/Idle_Clans/. I did not read individual reviews, so I can't say what drives the score.

### 6. Parallel actions, extra slots and followers
- **Melvor holds the one-active-skill line.** The forum shows a developer position: "Two actions at once is bad." One thread says the developer regrets an existing 2-action exception. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/4917340730756584239/. Strength: repeated, firm. Combat and woodcutting can't run together, and only Farming and Township are passive. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/591783706467828626/.
- **Melvor's sanctioned side-channels.** Summoning tablets earn XP while you do other things, and a couple of scroll or familiar synergies double up. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1267910/discussions/0/591783706467828626/ (the search summary lists the scroll and familiar synergies).
- **Players want it anyway.** There is a "Multitasking" mod, and players suggest hiring apprentices or buying upgrades with exponential costs. Sources: https://mod.io/g/melvoridle/m/multitasking and the multiple-actions thread above. Strength: repeated demand.
- **IdleOn's model.** Up to 10 characters, 0-1 active, 0-10 AFK. The dev states there will never be more than 1 active character, and AFK gains are often higher than active. A player also mentions 120% AFK gains. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1476970/discussions/0/3764480479602641804/. Strength: dev-stated rule. This is the strongest example of "more slots" without a chore treadmill. I did not find complaints about managing characters, so I can't say how chore-like it feels.
- **Increlution queues instead of parallelizing.** One review praises the queue for removing friction ("no punishment for thinking before acting"). Source: https://playwanderer.online/game-reviews/increlution. Strength: one review site, not player voices.
- **Magic Research apprentices** cast spells for you as an option. Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/2311680/discussions/0/3874843885717583371/. Weak, one comment.

### 7. Loadouts
- NGU Idle players keep named loadouts by purpose (gold drop, power/toughness, NGU/Hacks, seed farming, quest drops). Source: https://steamcommunity.com/app/1147690/discussions/0/3038229940682896482/. Strength: single thread, but the pattern is widely used per the game's community guides.

## What players love
- A boss that acts as a clear goal, where progress feels earned by preparing.
- Games that are "mostly idle until you hit a boss" that needs strategy.
- Queues and loadouts that let you plan once and then walk away (Increlution, NGU).
- Bonuses that stack from many sources (IdleOn's stamps, cards and alchemy).
- Optional idle-ability rewards at the end of a boss chain (Melvor's pets).

## What players dislike / complaints
- Owning gear they can't equip because of a dungeon gate (Melvor).
- New content that invalidates hard-earned gear (Melvor DLC).
- Bosses that can be triggered by accident with a large loss (Loop Hero).
- Boss ladders that outrun the actual progression (NGU's 301 bosses).
- Hard content that can't be idled and forces manual eating (Melvor's hardest dungeon).
- Wanting more parallelism while the developers refuse it (Melvor).

## Ideas and lessons for Ritual Idle
All of these fit the designer's rules (no real-time gating, no chores, no RNG failure, generous offline, no pay-to-win).

1. **Make each boss a "ritual" with a named prep puzzle, not a DPS check.** Give each spirit a weakness (salt for the restless dead, iron for the vodyanoy, and so on) and show it before the fight. That mirrors Melvor's phase-counter bosses, but as prep rather than mid-fight switching, which is the designer's stated core.
2. **Gate by "you may not proceed," never by losing stuff.** Loop Hero's accidental-boss loss is the anti-pattern. Boss attempts should be opt-in, with a clear readout of readiness. A loss should cost only the consumables you spent.
3. **Tie every boss to a real unlock** (a recipe, a village place, a new action, a Rite option). NGU's boss ladder shows a long list without unlocks feels like filler. Keep the count small and each meaningful.
4. **Let the boss unlock the gear, but show the gate up front.** Melvor's complaint is being able to own an item and not equip it. Show "beat the Drowned Bride to use this" at the recipe, before the player spends materials.
5. **Avoid gear replacement. Use upgrade paths and stacking bonuses.** Melvor's DLC replacement and IdleOn's stacking-bonus praise point the same way. Keep charms and sigils as slots that upgrade, and let old items stay useful through effects (a salt ring that keeps a niche use) instead of pure numbers.
6. **Crafting makes the gear, bosses make the rare inputs.** It matches Melvor's smithing plus boss-drop split and links combat to the existing skilling loop. Boss drops can be an ingredient rather than a finished item, so skilling stays necessary.
7. **Give a "manual mode" for bosses only as a bonus.** Melvor players get 0 deaths/hr by hand and a worse rate when idling. Do it the other way around: a boss must be beatable on auto with good prep. Manual eating or switching only shortens the time or improves rewards, the min-max layer the designer wants.
8. **More slots: follow IdleOn's rule, not Melvor's.** One active action, plus followers as passive AFK helpers that don't need managing. The dev's line that AFK gains often beat active gains is the model for "idle progresses." Consider an Increlution-style queue and saved loadouts instead of parallel play.
9. **Named saved loadouts.** Copy the NGU approach: a "Graveyard set" and a "Bog set", switched in one click. Combined with prep-once, this is the "simple yet deep" core.
10. **Offline combat vs. weaker foes** is a design decision, not something these sources answer directly. A natural approach is that only bosses need a present player and normal zones never do, but the sources here don't test it.

Gaps: no timed-versus-untimed boss evidence, thin Idle Clans player voices, and nothing on Anuke-style games or Idle Loops beyond a search snippet.
